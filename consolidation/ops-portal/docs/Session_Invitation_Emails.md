Nestled between Pittsburgh and the Chartiers Valley in the Ohio River lies a 5-mile long strip of land. Originally called Montour's Island and later Long Island, it was renamed Neville Island in honor of Revolutionary War General John Neville. It was once farmland renowned as the "Market Basket of Pittsburgh," which all changed at the turn of the century. Construction of the first bridges and electric street railway allowed easy access to the mainland. American Steel & Wire Co began construction on the island's first mill in 1900. During WWI, the US Government acquired 130 acres for a munitions plant in 1918. After Pearl Harbor, the Dravo Corporation was offered a contract by the US Navy to produce 300 ships for the war effort. This cemented the industrial development of Neville Island. Today the 1200 acre township is characterized by a unique mix of heavy industry, logistics, and a small, tight-knit residential community hosting active manufacturing, chemical plants, and operating under a climate of ongoing environmental remediation and redevelopment.

American Steel & Wire put that mill on former farmland at the east end of the island, a blast furnace reached by the company's own railroad, the Pittsburgh & Ohio Valley. Davison Coke & Iron took the furnace and grew the rest of the works around it: coke ovens beside the stack, and a cement plant run on the slag and on stone brought up the river. Chemicals drawn from the oven gas and tar carried the company from Pittsburgh Coke & Iron to Pittsburgh Coke & Chemical — PC&C — and in wartime the same coal became activated carbon, standing in for coconut shell in military gas masks. The industries we switch there are what that one plant became. Shenango kept the coke and iron, Kosmos the cement, Aristech the chemicals after a turn through U.S. Steel, and Calgon Carbon the carbon.

From the Pittsburgh & Ohio Central notes: two jobs start the day around 07:30 at the McKees Rocks office. Both jobs head from McKees Rocks to Duff Junction, where they interchange cars with the NS local. Both jobs then sort the cars at the junction and/or at Scully Yard. One job heads back through McKees Rocks and up to Neville Island to work there. The other job, the Arden/Carnegie Turn, runs south to either work the branch to Arden (Monday, Wednesday, and Friday) or work the short branch at Carnegie (Tuesday and Thursday).

On our railroad, the HART Railroad manages the interchange between CSX and POHC and the work of the local industries. We focus on the Neville Island turn (NVL): classification in South Yard, receiving and sending from Duff Junction, and the POV interchange with CSX.

CSX local D749 works out of Neville Island, heading east towards Demmler Yard, swapping blocks on the way down to McKeesport and back. The POHC works out of McKees Rocks around noon, heading to Neville Island. Often they wait for D749 to clear the trestle over the Ohio River, and for clearance from the CSX dispatcher. The POHC works some shared sidings before switching chemical industries and a fuel transload for the airport. There are a number of industries dealing with scrap metal and metal fabrication. There is a steel building fabricator, which POHC delivers girders and sheet steel on flat cars, entering the building. Visitors should be wary of the truck traffic — dump trucks and 18 wheelers — and the no trespassing signs.

The NVL comes in to switch out the local industries before heading back to McKees Rocks and tying down in Scully Yard. The coke works is constantly sending imbalanced or overloaded hoppers, so we weigh the outgoing coke shipment to make sure the loads are properly distributed and within tolerance. The track scale has been serviced, so it needs calibrating before the first weigh. After much local demand, we've refurbished a classic PCC streetcar for commuter service, recreating the Coraopolis–Sewickley route. A CSX track inspection train may do a run through before the streetcar's PM service.
