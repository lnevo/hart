Here's a bit of background and updated history to refresh you on the railroad.

Nestled between Pittsburgh and the Chartiers Valley in the Ohio River lies a 5-mile long strip of land. Originally called Montour's Island and later Long Island, it was renamed Neville Island in honor of Revolutionary War General John Neville. It was once farmland renowned as the "Market Basket of Pittsburgh," which all changed at the turn of the century. Construction of the first bridges and electric street railway allowed easy access to the mainland. American Steel & Wire Co began construction on the island's first mill in 1900. The blast furnace was set on former farmland at the east end of the island and served by the company's own railroad, the Pittsburgh & Ohio Valley.

Davison Coke & Iron bought the furnace in 1929 and built coke ovens and a cement plant beside it. The cement was made from the furnace slag, with limestone brought up the river. The company went bankrupt in the Depression and was reorganized in 1936 as Pittsburgh Coke & Iron. It added chemicals from the coke-oven gas and tar around 1940, and in 1942 became Pittsburgh Coke & Chemical (PC&C). That same year the government asked PC&C for a substitute for coconut-shell carbon in gas masks, and the plant started making activated carbon from bituminous coal. PC&C was later split into the plants that came out of that works: Shenango got the coke and iron, Green Bag cement became Kosmos, USS Chemicals became Aristech, and Calgon Carbon got the carbon business. The POV ceased operations in the early 1990s, which is where we come in.

HART manages the trains on the island — local industry switching, interchange, and commuter transit — aimed at a year from 2008 to 2016, possibly later, with locomotives and cars that lag that date. The island is served by CSX train D749, the Pittsburgh & Ohio Central NVL, and the restored Pittsburgh Railways' Coraopolis-Sewickley Route 23 PCC streetcar. We'll need someone for each of those trains, as well as a dispatcher, yardmaster, and someone for the coke works.
