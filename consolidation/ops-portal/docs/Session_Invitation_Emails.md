Nestled between Pittsburgh and the Chartiers Valley in the Ohio River lies a 5-mile long strip of land. Originally called Montour's Island and later Long Island, it was renamed Neville Island in honor of Revolutionary War General John Neville. It was once farmland renowned as the "Market Basket of Pittsburgh," which all changed at the turn of the century. Construction of the first bridges and electric street railway allowed easy access to the mainland. American Steel & Wire Co began construction on the island's first mill in 1900. The blast furnace, was set on former farmland at the east end of the island and served by the company's own railroad, the Pittsburgh & Ohio Valley. Davison Coke & Iron bought the furnace in 1929 and built coke ovens and a cement plant beside it. The cement was made from the furnace slag, with limestone brought up the river. The company went bankrupt in the Depression and was reorganized in 1936 as Pittsburgh Coke & Iron. It added chemicals from the coke-oven gas and tar around 1940, and in 1942 became Pittsburgh Coke & Chemical (PC&C). That same year the government asked PC&C for a substitute for coconut-shell carbon in gas masks, and the plant started making activated carbon from bituminous coal. PC&C was later split into the plants that came out of that works: Shenango got the coke and iron, Green Bag cement became Kosmos, USS Chemicals became Aristech, and Calgon Carbon got the carbon business. The POV ceased operations in the early 1990s, which is where we come in.

On our railroad, the HART Railroad manages the interchange between CSX and POHC and the work of the local industries. We focus on the Neville Island turn (NVL): classification in South Yard, receiving and sending from Duff Junction, and the POV interchange with CSX.

From the Pittsburgh & Ohio Central notes: two jobs start the day around 07:30 at the McKees Rocks office. Both jobs head from McKees Rocks to Duff Junction, where they interchange cars with the NS local. Both jobs then sort the cars at the junction and/or at Scully Yard. One job heads back through McKees Rocks and up to Neville Island to work there. The other job, the Arden/Carnegie Turn, runs south to either work the branch to Arden (Monday, Wednesday, and Friday) or work the short branch at Carnegie (Tuesday and Thursday).

CSX local D749 works out of Neville Island, heading east towards Demmler Yard, swapping blocks on the way down to McKeesport and back. The POHC works out of McKees Rocks around noon, heading to Neville Island. Often they wait for D749 to clear the trestle over the Ohio River, and for clearance from the CSX dispatcher. The POHC works some shared sidings before switching chemical industries and a fuel transload for the airport. There are a number of industries dealing with scrap metal and metal fabrication. There is a steel building fabricator, which POHC delivers girders and sheet steel on flat cars, entering the building. Visitors should be wary of the truck traffic — dump trucks and 18 wheelers — and the no trespassing signs.

The NVL comes in to switch out the local industries before heading back to McKees Rocks and tying down in Scully Yard. The coke works is constantly sending imbalanced or overloaded hoppers, so we weigh the outgoing coke shipment to make sure the loads are properly distributed and within tolerance. The track scale has been serviced, so it needs calibrating before the first weigh.

Route 23 is the streetcar. After much local demand, we've refurbished a classic PCC for commuter service, recreating the Coraopolis–Sewickley route. There is an AM trip and a PM trip. Freight holds until the streetcar clears. A CSX track inspection train may do a run through before the PM service.
