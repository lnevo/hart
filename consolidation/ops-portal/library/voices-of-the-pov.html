Voices of the P&OV

Crew, sayings, and yard-office life on the Pittsburgh & Ohio Valley, 1977–1991 · October 2026

Summarized from Mark Connolly’s Tumblr blog. Everything on this page comes from @pittsburghohiovalleyrailroad, where Mark, a P&OV trainman and engineer from 1977 to 1991, has posted his photos and memories of the railroad since 2013.

We condensed his captions into short profiles of the people he worked with. The quotes are his words, kept as he wrote them, and every “photo” or “post” link opens the original on Tumblr. For the full stories and pictures, read the blog.

The Pittsburgh & Ohio Valley Railway was a small railroad, and the people on it knew each other well. Names and jobs below are as Mark gave them in his captions. Stanley Quick and John Simpson come from comments collected by Dennis DeBruler.

Family details from the obituaries he reposted are left out. Only the railroad careers are kept.

In their own words

“Tell em nothing and practice telling em even less.”

— Elmer Picard, conductor (photo, Tumblr post)

“Leave it like you found it.”

— Elmer Picard (photo)

“If I could have thrown that switch you two would have both been up on the road.”

— Bill McEwen Sr., early 1980s, when Mark Connolly and John Iwanonkiw could not throw the frozen C9 switch at −10 °F. “In this case we really could not throw the switch over.” (Tumblr post)

“How come railroad employees are not guaranteed 40 hours a week” — “Cause we were on the extra board for all those years!”

— John Iwanonkiw's answer when a 1988 photo asked for a candid comment (photo)

The crews

Joe “Chick Chick” Adamcik (Joe Dates) Conductor and brakeman

On the #105 job he was supposed to ride the fifth car of the ore drag, “but we all know that if you hide in the cab you become invisible and no one in the yard office can see you.” When the crew chipped in for the crew room's first microwave, he heated potatoes and kept them in his pockets to warm his hands, eating one when he got hungry. “He sure did smile and make us smile a lot.” He also told Mark about the 1945 carbon plant explosion. (photo, photo, photo, photo, Tumblr post, Tumblr post)

Elmer Edward Picard Conductor, “the legend”

Worked the steady second-trick #205 job or the #115 relief job, and came to work every day in a sport coat and tie. “Looked like he owned the place when he filled out his time sheet.” He held his paychecks for long stretches to annoy the accountants. After a 1957 injury settlement, in which he testified he could “probably be an elevator operator,” he worked another 30 years on the railroad. At Thanksgiving the mill handed out frozen turkeys; Elmer threatened to throw his off the Fleming Park Bridge “and see if it will fly,” then gave it away. (photo, Tumblr post, photo, photo)

John Iwanonkiw Conductor, trainman, and car rider

Part of the “Southside crew.” He bled gons and ladles at the PC&Y Interchange, worked the #105 and #110 jobs, and rode strings of cars down from the car dumper on E2, braking by hand. Many of the blog's dumper and scale-house photos are his. (photo, photo, photo, photo, photo, photo)

Sam Lacenere Conductor

Rode the lead hopper of furnace coke strings to F1, and worked the South Yard and the dumper. “If your car needed brake work Sam was the man. Great guy.” (photo, photo, photo, photo)

Nick Niapas Conductor

Worked the #105 and sometimes the #110 daylight jobs. “Always pleasant to work with.” Pictured with Wally Latshaw at the end of a #105 shift: “You can tell by the smiles that it must have been an early quit!” (photo, photo, Tumblr post)

Wally Latshaw Conductor

Shoved boxcars over the scale and coupled empty ladles at the Y Interchange, from 1980 to at least 1990. (photo, photo, photo)

Don Amber and Bob “The Doctor” Paulin Conductor and brakeman

Photographed having coffee near the coke plant lead while working “the cherished #105 job on the ‘clean end’ of the plant,” in the green P&OV hats the whole crew wore. “The third coffee is mine.” (photo, photo)

Larry Wilcox Southside crew

Switched the West Yard with Don Amber in 1979, and was part of the Southside connection with John Iwanonkiw and Billy Bobak. (photo, photo)

Larry Obusek #115 relief job

Pictured on SW9 #6 working the #115 relief job in August 1979. “Larry was probably the nicest guy on the railroad. No one had a bad word to say about him.” He was still on the railroad in 1988, pictured with Loco #4 on the South Runner. (photo, Tumblr post, photo)

Kevin Connolly Track gang supervisor and trainman

In January 1977 the union filed a grievance against the company for having Kevin Connolly, then the track gang supervisor, run “the Dragon.” Early-1980s photos show a Kevin Connolly working the #105 job with Nick Niapas and Wally Latshaw on Loco #4, and at the gin game in the back room of the scale house. The captions do not say whether he moved from the track gang to the train crews. (photo, photo, photo)

Tom Connolly Car knocker

Worked his way through college inspecting and repairing cars on the P&OV in the 1980s. Kevin and Denny Connolly also appear on the blog; it was a railroad of family connections. (photo, photo, photo)

Engineers

John Doubt Engineer

Started on the P&OV in 1972, worked the #110 Furnace Job a lot, and later ran for CSX until 2010. (photo, Tumblr post, photo, photo)

Jack Burik Engineer

Pictured at the throttle of P&LE 1554 “with the throttle and enthusiasm both at notch 7.” In 1987 he found a Shenango track crane hung up with five loads on the East Runner Main, which became a grievance the union won. (photo, photo, Tumblr post)

Scott Morrison Engineer

Third out on the extra board most of the time, he added water to Loco #4 “the hard way” at the scale house in 1988. He boxed as a left-handed light heavyweight; a group of the crew went to see him fight. “He went the distance but lost the decision.” (photo)

Billy Bobak Engineer

Ran Loco #3 toward the scale house as Santa Claus in December 1988. He also worked for the Union Railroad. (photo)

David Drost Engineer

A P&OV engineer who retired from Shenango and was a 40-year member of the United Transportation Union. He died in July 2015. (Tumblr post)

Mike Britzki Engineer, “of the Rocks”

Pictured taking a break on the engineer's side of the cab, and once at the controls while Walter Cooper hitched a ride to the ore dumper: “10 shades of wrong here with conductor and engineer changing roles.” (photo, photo)

Trainmasters and the yard office

Wayne Amber, Bill McEwen, and Denny Jarvis The weighers

Trainmaster Wayne Amber, Bill McEwen, and Trainmaster Denny Jarvis did the weighing at the scale house. “I was always amazed at how fast he, Bill McEwen, and Denny Jarvis could weigh anything in a heartbeat. You no sooner cut away and backed off the scale and bam the buzzer was off.” Jarvis also wrote the crews' switching orders. Mark bought and framed a print of Loco #3 and the yard office; at Jarvis's funeral there was a photo of him holding the same picture. (photo, photo, photo, Tumblr post)

Walt Cooper Yard clerk

“#1 Daylight yard office clerk!” Walt was “always walking the yard and plant.” He sometimes rode up to the dumper with a crew, but usually walked, knocking on the cars to tell the loads from the empties. “Great gin player, button box man … A really kind man.” (photo, photo, photo)

Jack Burik Sr. Clerk

Clerked in the evenings and summers when he was not teaching or coaching. He had taught Mark business law in high school, so “he will always be Mr. Burik to me.” (Tumblr post)

Chauncey Evans Trainmaster

Retired in 1978 as trainmaster after a 30-year career on the P&OV. The blog also shows his October 1970 paycheck. (Tumblr post, photo)

Track gang and Shenango neighbors

Paul Hall Shenango high-lift operator

Ran the high lift at the Shenango car dumper, and is pictured there talking with John Iwanonkiw. Posting a January 1993 photo of a plant accident, in which a payloader hit a plant truck, Mark wrote: “I remember Paul Hall, he was a good operator and always was aware of us taking drags to dumper.” (photo, photo)

Rob Dibish Shenango crane operator

Running the Shenango track crane in 1987 when Jack Burik found it hung up with five loads on the East Runner Main. (photo)

Ron Makowski Furnace worker, of Rook Station

Pictured working one of the last casts at Shenango B furnace. (photo)

Denny Shazer Pictured with the track gang

In the flannel shirt in a 1980 photo of the track gang loading sand into Loco #3 by the bucket on S9, the last job of the #305 crew. (photo)

Stanley Quick 38 years at Shenango

Spent “the best 38 years of my life” at Shenango Inc. He remembered the furnaces coming down in stages and said of P&OV #4 at the foundry: “I used to work on that engine.” (post)

John Simpson PC&Y

Worked on the PC&Y and recalled hauling ore to Neville Island out of Scully Yard. (post)

Around the yard office

  • Coffee runs: crews stopped at the millwrights' shop on the furnace lead after a drag to the dumper, around 10:00 to 10:30 a.m., and brought coffee back to the yard office. A four-cup holder hung on the bulletin board. (photo)
  • Gin games in the back room of the scale house in the 1980s. (photo)
  • The yard office itself: a Toledo scale, a lantern, base radio, phones, track cards, a hardhat, and a horseshoe above the door. “Yep, that's a yard office alright.” (photo)
  • Foot-board relief: crews coming on duty sometimes relieved the engineer to weigh cars or take a drag to the dumper, so the earlier crew could get a slightly earlier quit. (photo)
  • Paperwork: a locomotive time card, on which Mark's favorite entry was “ALLOW OUTLAW 1 HOUR FOR LUNCH,” a scale ticket, union letterhead, and P&OV parking passes, “one for the Olds and one for the Pontiac.” (photo, photo, photo)
  • Coffee breaks at the gandydancer shed off the west end of S1. (photo)

UTU Local 150

  • P&OV crews belonged to United Transportation Union Local 150, whose officers helped with grievances, law boards, safety issues, and later the merger hurdles with the P&LE and CSX. (photo)
  • Tony Prozzley, whose nomination for the local's vice president Mark was told to second at his first meeting, had UTU Local 150 hats made up for the crew. (photo, photo)
  • The Scully Yard picnic, around 1980, brought out John Iwanonkiw, Bill McEwen, Mark Connolly, Larry Wilcox, Bob Paulin, Sam Lacenere, and Joe Adamcik. (photo, photo)
  • Grievances included a January 1977 claim over a track gang supervisor running “the Dragon,” and the 1987 crane hung up on the East Runner, an “easy win for UTU.” (photo, photo)
  • Local 150 drew its officers from several roads. Tim Finnerty was General Chairman on the P&WV and Jim McCaffery a Conrail Local Chairman. Mark counted two Finnertys, a McCaffery, Dunleavy, Connolly, and Mackey among the local's officers; Tim Finnerty and Don Dunleavy were the ones who helped the P&OV crews. (photo)
  • Mark kept his 1988 P&OV benefits book and a 1969 Shenango Pension Agreement book. The steelworker book still had the mill dust on it: “That stuff is still in our lungs!” Years later Mark noted that P&OV employees had vested in the Shenango pension: “ERISA worked!” (photo, photo)
  • Jim McCaffery of Local 150 always worked the Santa Train in Carnegie. (photo)
  • Mark Connolly and Don Dunleavy represented the local at the 1991 UTU convention. (photo)

Remembered

  • August 3, 1945: six men died in an explosion at the carbon plant. Chick Chick told Mark about it years later. (Tumblr post)
  • September 1945: one man dead and one injured in a blast at Pittsburgh Coke & Chemical. Mark's post names Clifford Thompson. (Tumblr post)
  • July 1953: William Hutchins was killed by a ladle at the foundry. (Tumblr post)
  • 1960: two Shenango blast furnace employees, Ron Minnick and Frank Gottschalk, were killed. (Tumblr post)
  • Early 1960s: a welder died at the Marquette Cement plant. (Tumblr post)
  • May 20, 1972: Anthony Papst was killed at Shenango. (Tumblr post)
  • March 1987: crane operator Ollie Carlini died in a fall at the ladle house, where electricians had left an access grate open. (Tumblr post)
  • December 1985: a gandydancer from West Virginia was killed at the Shenango battery. (Tumblr post)
