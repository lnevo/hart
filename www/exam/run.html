Extra West

A game for the C&E exam. You are the conductor of a westbound freight extra on the Kestrel Valley, an invented railroad, mostly single track, and you take it 46 miles from Ashby to Ironton. Pick the rules era at the start. Each decision uses a rule from the study deck, and the source is shown after every answer.

1949: Time-table and train orders

Extra 812 West has no schedule and gives way to every regular train. You read the time-table, decide where to wait for opposing and following trains, pick up train orders at the open offices, and protect the train when it stops on the main.

Rulebook: 1949 Standard Code

Modern: Track warrant control

KV 812 West runs in dark territory on track warrants. You copy each warrant the dispatcher reads, repeat it back, wait for the OK time, and stop at the right switch at the end of your limits.

Rulebook: GCOR

How a run works
  • The run has eight legs between Ashby and Ironton. Each leg brings a decision or an event on the road.
  • A wrong call costs time, a mark against your record, or both. Five marks and you are pulled from service. A few mistakes, such as a collision or running past your authority, end the run at once.
  • At Ironton the run is scored on time, marks and what is left in the caboose. Your missed rules are listed with links back to the study guide.
  • Today's run gives everyone the same time-table, orders and events for the day. Your first try counts, and you can copy the result to compare with friends.
  • The 1949 run uses the Standard Code's 5-minute clearing margin (Rule S-87). The TT&TO primer quotes 10; see the deck's Guide notes.
