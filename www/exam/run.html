Extra East

A game for the C&E exam on the Kestrel Valley, an invented railroad, mostly single track. You are the conductor of an eastbound freight extra and take it 46 miles from Ashby to Ironton, or you work a shift on the Ironton yard job. Pick a run at the start. Each decision uses a rule from the study deck, and the source is shown after every answer.

1949: Time-table and train orders

Extra 812 East has no schedule and gives way to every regular train. You read the time-table, decide where to wait for opposing and following trains, pick up train orders at the open offices, and protect the train when it stops on the main.

Rulebook: 1949 Standard Code

Modern: Track warrant control

KV 812 East runs in dark territory on track warrants. You copy each warrant the dispatcher reads, repeat it back, wait for the OK time, and stop at the right switch at the end of your limits.

Rulebook: GCOR

Signals: Ashby to Ironton

The same 46 miles on a signaled line. Read each signal as it comes up, run at the speed it allows, and do what its plate says when it shows Stop. Switch between the 1949 speed semaphores and modern route signals.

Rulebook: 1949 Standard Code Rules 281 to 292

Yard job: Ironton yard limits

A short shift on the Ironton yard job. You pull cars, use the main track inside yard limits, spot loads at Kestrel Feed and keep out of the way of trains, working for the yardmaster.

Rulebook: GCOR

How a run works
  • The 1949, modern and signals runs have eight legs between Ashby and Ironton. Each leg brings a decision, an event or a signal on the road. The yard job is a shorter shift inside the Ironton yard limits.
  • A wrong call costs time, a mark against your record, or both. Five marks and you are pulled from service. A few mistakes, such as a collision or running past your authority, end the run at once.
  • At the end the run is scored on time and marks, and the 1949 run also on what is left in the caboose. Your missed rules are listed with links back to the study guide.
  • A practice run is a new draw every time. Today's run gives everyone the same time-table, orders, signals and events for the day; your first try counts, and you can copy the result to compare with friends. The signals run has a daily run for each signal system.
  • The 1949 run uses the Standard Code's 5-minute clearing margin (Rule S-87). The TT&TO primer quotes 10; see the deck's Guide notes.
