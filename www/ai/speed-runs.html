The job

Two locomotives that run at different speeds cannot work together; one drags the other. I wanted CSX 2121 and 2738 to run as a pair.

What AI did

A program running inside JMRI sets a locomotive to a throttle step, lets it run through the block sensors and times each block, step after step, with no hands on the throttle. Block lengths now come from the track plan and were checked against timed passes. Both locomotives were measured in both directions on 13 November 2026, 2738 was adjusted to match 2121, and the two were compared stopping.

The 13 November 2026 runs

2738 now runs within 4 per cent of 2121 at every step from 24 up, in both pairings of the two locomotives (most steps within 2 to 3 per cent). Step 16 is 2 per cent fast in one pairing and 6 per cent fast in the other. The locomotives face away from each other in a consist, so 2121 forward runs with 2738 reverse and the other way round.

Speeds are in mm/s, as written to the roster. The tolerance is 5 per cent: one step repeats to about 2 per cent between runs, and to 6 per cent at the slowest steps. Step 8 is creep, where 2738 is about 20 per cent fast and stays so.

2121 ran 3 to 10 per cent faster in reverse than forward, while 2738 ran evenly both ways. Raising only 2738’s forward trim brought 2738 forward up to 2121 reverse; the other pairing already matched.

Stopping from step 32 and from step 96 on the same track, with the same trigger, every block lit within 0.2 seconds of the other locomotive, and both came to rest in the same block. 2121 slows more gently than its settings predict, and 2738 matches it. The test of the coupled pair did not run cleanly, so the solo match stands for it.

Both roster entries now hold the measured speed profile, eight points each. 2121 was not changed.

The first run, 8 November 2026

The first program set each locomotive to a throttle step and timed laps of the whole loop. Both topped out at about the same speed, but 2738 ran at only 47 to 74 per cent of 2121’s speed through the lower steps. Its settings explained why: the speed table was switched off and it had no momentum.

2738 was matched to 2121, momentum included, with a calculated speed table programmed on the programming track; every value read back correctly. Measured again at steps 24, 48 and 126 it ran within 1.2 per cent of 2121, and about 5 per cent fast at step 12. At step 96 the two stopped within a tenth of a second of each other and started within three tenths. The two were timed running in opposite directions then, which the 13 November runs corrected.

    How well it went

    What came out

    A matched pair, ready to be tried coupled, and a method to repeat for the other fifteen locomotives.

    Verdict
    • Worked well: hours of unattended, repeatable measuring, a speed table worked out from the numbers rather than by feel, and one trim setting that matched both directions.
    • Be honest about: step 8 stays about 20 per cent apart, and step 16 is 6 per cent apart in one pairing. Block lengths now come from the track plan and were checked against timed passes; both directions were measured. Some loop 2 lengths are still estimates, so scale miles an hour is approximate. A block lighting again behind the locomotive can be taken as the next block, which is not fixed. The coupled stop test has not run cleanly.
