Master Model Railroader (MMR) is the NMRA’s top award: seven of the twelve Achievement Program certificates. This page covers the program, the checklists that keep the score, the operations log, the Electrical SOQ package and the applications, all built from the railroad’s own records.

The Achievement Program

The National Model Railroad Association (NMRA) runs the Achievement Program (AP). It has twelve certificates in four groups:

  • Equipment: Master Builder – Cars, and Master Builder – Motive Power.
  • Setting: Master Builder – Structures, Scenery, Prototype Models and Modular Railroading.
  • Construction & Operation: Model Railroad Engineer – Electrical and Civil, and Chief Dispatcher.
  • Service: Model Railroad Author, Association Volunteer and Association Official.

Earn seven of the twelve, with at least one from each group, and you are a Master Model Railroader (MMR). You claim each certificate with a Statement of Qualification (SOQ): a form and the evidence, sent to the Region AP Manager (the volunteer who runs the program for the Region) by way of your Division, the local NMRA chapter. Models and scenes need a Merit Award: 87.5 of 125 points from judges at a contest or an AP evaluation. Layout certificates such as Electrical need a witness instead: an AP volunteer who visits the layout and signs that it meets the rules.

The NMRA’s Operations Special Interest Group (OPSIG) started its own program for operators in 2025. Each of its Achievements stands alone, with its own certificate. Earn eight, including Conductor & Engineer, and you are a Ninja (Qualified Operator).

In November 2026 HART had none of the twelve yet, but several were well along. Model Railroad Engineer – Electrical was ready for a witness, Chief Dispatcher was more than half done, and Author had its first formal point. The live score is on the checklist pages below.

The job

The requirements are spread over a dozen NMRA pages, plus OPSIG’s own, each with its own rules about what counts. The checklists put it in one place: what each certificate needs, what the railroad already has, and what is left.

What AI did

It read each published requirement and wrote it as one checklist line, linked to the rule it came from. Then it matched every line against what the railroad already has and linked the evidence on this site: the track plan, the station maps, the tables file, the block detectors, the articles. Each line is marked Done, Ready (paperwork complete, waiting for the witness), Partly, To do, or Confirm for anything kept outside these records. A dashboard at the top of each page reads its scores from the checklists, so the two cannot drift apart, and tests check the rules that turn marks into scores.

What came out

An NMRA page with all twelve certificates, a path to seven that covers all four groups, and a “what to do first” list. An OPSIG page does the same toward Ninja. Much of the evidence already existed: 43 detected sections for Electrical, a scale track plan for Civil, and enough website articles to reach the 21-point cap on informal work for Author.

Verdict
  • Worked well: finding evidence the railroad already had, and linking each claim to both the rule and the proof.
  • Needed me: facts only I know. My memberships, that A&W, the club I belong to, is my home club (its sessions do not count for OPSIG Conductor & Engineer), and that the first STS session only loaded cars into a new database, so four sessions count toward Host, not five.

The job

Chief Dispatcher needs 50 hours operating on layouts in several jobs. OPSIG’s Conductor & Engineer, Yardmaster and Boomer Achievements count sessions too. Before the log, my record of two years of visits was a private list of notes.

What AI did

It typed the notes into a log page in month order, with hours totalled by job, and left out the names of layout owners and the people I went with. It found one session logged twice and merged it. The same totals feed both checklists, so each program reads the same hours.

What came out

34 sessions and 108 hours on other layouts, 81 of them as conductor and engineer. That covers the Chief Dispatcher hours apart from the 10 needed as dispatcher. Sessions hosted on HART are listed apart, as the record for OPSIG Host.

Verdict
  • Worked well: one log that both programs read from, with the totals worked out from the entries.
  • Limit: a few sessions were logged without a month.

The job

The Electrical certificate asks for the NMRA form, a description of each item claimed, and schematics of the track power circuit and of each item. The railroad already had wiring slide decks, a system schematic and the tables file, but none of it was in the shape the program asks for.

What AI did

It chose the items that fit the railroad: powered turnout frogs, crossovers and two reversing sections for the trackwork, and three control items: turnout indication, block detection and the bridge that connects the computer to the railroad. It ticked them on the NMRA’s own fillable form and wrote the description. A script draws six schematics (D1 to D6: power and control, a typical detected section, turnout frog wiring, a crossover with its position indication, a reversing section, and the computer interface) from the railroad’s schematic and my answers about the wiring, and everything is put together as one PDF. My name, address and NMRA number are left off. They go only on a private copy.

What came out

A complete Electrical SOQ package, apart from my name, address and signature. The certificate shows as Ready, waiting only for the Region AP Manager or a designee to look at the layout and sign.

Verdict
  • Worked well: turning the railroad’s own documents into the program’s format, with drawings that are redrawn from the data whenever a fact changes.
  • Needed me: checking the drawings against the real wiring. The first frog drawing had the gaps on the wrong rails and the crossover rails were not parallel, and I had to correct how the feeders pass through each detector.

The job

Each program has its own forms, and some need another person to confirm the claim.

What AI did

It drafted the OPSIG Yardmaster application from the log, ready for me to add the sentences about each role and the contacts who can confirm them; the draft is kept privately because it holds those details. It worked out that the STS article published in The Cannonball, the Sunrise Trails Division newsletter, counts as the first formal point for NMRA Author and the first of two articles for OPSIG Author. And it wrote the next step under every certificate, in the order to work on them.

What came out

No application has been sent yet. The next steps, in order: a witness for Electrical; 10 hours as dispatcher, a freight timetable and a train chart for Chief Dispatcher; for OPSIG, the Conductor & Engineer exam and one more session hosted on HART to complete Host; and more articles and clinics for Author. Civil is planned into the main level, where three hand-laid turnouts can be built in.

Verdict
  • Worked well: a next step listed for every certificate.
  • Needed me: everything people judge. The signatures, the witnesses, the contacts and the models themselves stay mine.
