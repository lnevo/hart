GETTING STARTED

Four habits

1 Start with something you know

Ask about a locomotive or a technique you know well, so you can judge the answers.

2 Give it the whole picture

Say your scale, era, what you’re modelling and what you have, and whether you want a list, a table or a plan back.

3 Work in small steps

Ask for one thing at a time and check it before asking for the next.

4 Write down what you decide

It doesn’t remember earlier chats. Keep a short note of your choices and paste it in when you start again.

PROJECT 1 · PROTOTYPE RESEARCH

Choosing the place and era

THE JOB

I had a roster of equipment and an empty room, but no prototype.

WHAT AI DID

Went through the roster with me, asked what to model and when, and compared candidate corridors. I made the choice.

WHAT CAME OUT

Neville Island, in the Ohio River below Pittsburgh, with plastics, carbon, cement, propane and a coke works to switch. The track plan was settled in July 2025.

Speaker notes

About 4 minutes. Tool: ChatGPT, spring to summer 2025. After the choice was made, it also helped write the fictional backstory that sits on the real geography.

PROJECT 2 · CONSTRUCTION

Planning the helix

THE JOB

The helix climbs from staging to the main level. It needed a gentle grade and clearance under each turn, and had to be built from plywood by one person.

WHAT AI DID

Worked out the radius, grade, clearance, roadbed cuts and build order with me before any wood was cut.

WHAT CAME OUT

A 13-page construction plan, and a design record that lists the options considered and why they were turned down.

Speaker notes

About 3 minutes. Tool: ChatGPT, September 2026. The plan held through the build; later corrections were about the materials, not the design.

PROJECT 2 · CONSTRUCTION

Printed fixtures for the helix

Router jigs

Templates for the half-lap joints and bracket pockets, with a depth block and gauge.

Router base plate

Fits the router to the circle jig that cut the helix arcs.

Risers

Piers under the first loop and risers under the entry liftout, set to the grade.

Cork transition bracket

Holds the helix where the corked track meets the plywood, so the railheads line up.

Also printed: height spacers that set each bracket 1/2″ higher every 45°, and spacers that check the gap between loops. All sized from the measured 0.72″ plywood.

Speaker notes

About 3 minutes. Tool: Claude, September to October 2026. Pass a fixture around. Claude turned the plan into scripts and the scripts into printable files, and checked each file before printing. The height spacers stack like building blocks to mark the top of the roadbed at each bracket. The cork transition bracket makes up the 0.236-inch step between the liftout and the first helix piece. The first height template was settled at 1-1/32 inch by test fit. One batch of corner spacers printed 1.65 mm short, so a measured correction went in.

PROJECT 3 · OPERATIONS

Car cards from photos

THE JOB

I had a roster and photographs of the fleet, but no car cards.

WHAT AI DID

Cropped each photo, read the reporting marks and the small stencils, such as built dates and weights, and matched them to the roster.

WHAT CAME OUT

A printed card for every car with its own picture, and waybills for the layout’s industries.

Speaker notes

About 4 minutes with the next slide. Pass the cards around. Stencilled lettering is hard for a computer to read, so any number it is unsure of goes to me to check.

PROJECT 3 · OPERATIONS

The car-cards plugin

  1. Take a photo of each car
  2. AI crops it and reads the numbers
  3. You check every reading on a review page
  4. Print car and locomotive cards, nine to a sticker sheet

The plugin is free and works with Claude Code, Cursor or Codex. Details are at hartrailroad.com/ai.

Speaker notes

The workflow I used became this plugin. Nothing prints until a person has checked the review page.

PROJECT 4 · LOCOMOTIVES

Speed matching CSX 2121 and 2738

THE JOB

2121 and 2738 run as a pair, so 2738’s decoder had to match 2121’s speeds.

WHAT AI DID

Wrote a program in JMRI that ran each locomotive through the block detectors at each throttle step and timed every block, then adjusted 2738’s decoder.

WHAT CAME OUT

2738 runs within 4% of 2121 from step 24 up, in both directions, and the two stop together.

Speaker notes

About 4 minutes. Tool: Claude, November 2026. The runs were unattended. The slowest steps still differ, and the first coupled run is still to come. Every recorded run is on the website.
