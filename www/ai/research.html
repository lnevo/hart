Research was the first thing I asked AI to do, in early 2025, before there was any code or much benchwork. It is also where AI needs the most watching, because a confident answer about history can be wrong in ways that are hard to spot.

01Choosing the place and era

Tool
ChatGPT
When
March to summer 2025
Status
Done

The job

I had a roster of equipment on hand and a room to fill. I needed a real corridor to interpret, an era that suited the equipment, and a backstory that explained why a small railroad would be switching these industries.

What AI did

ChatGPT worked as a research partner. It went through the roster with me, raised the questions of what to model and in which era, and helped compare candidate corridors. Once the choice was made it helped write the fictional backstory that sits on top of the real geography.

What came out

Neville Island and the railroads around it, with the industries crews now switch: plastics, carbon, cement, propane and a coke works on the mainland. The track plan was settled in July 2025 on that basis.

Verdict
  • Worked well: laying out options quickly and asking the questions I had not thought to ask.
  • Needed me: the choice itself. AI can list corridors; it cannot tell you which one you will enjoy operating for years.

02Plant and railroad histories

Tool
ChatGPT, then Claude
When
Second half of 2025, ongoing
Status
Ongoing

The job

To run the industries believably I needed to know what each plant made, who owned it when, and which railroad served it. Neville Island's plants changed hands many times, and so did its railroads.

What AI did

It searched, summarised and drafted. Ownership chains were worked out step by step, and each article was written from the sources found. Later drafts were read through a historian's eyes by a reviewer helper, which asks where each claim comes from.

What came out

The articles on the Prototype pages, from the coke, cement and chemical history to the industries along the line from McKees Rocks to Washington, and two charts that follow the coke works and the railroads owner by owner.

Verdict
  • Worked well: turning scattered sources into a readable sequence, and keeping long ownership chains straight.
  • Needed me: checking. A summary is only as good as its source, so the articles cite theirs.

03The historic archive

Tool
ChatGPT, then a Claude research helper
When
2025 to now
Status
Ongoing

The job

Old photographs, advertisements, clippings, timetables and maps turn up from many places. Each needs a caption, a date if one can be found, and above all a source.

What AI did

An early pass used ChatGPT's image reading to sort and describe a large folder of old documents for review on a local page. Today a research helper does the looking. It searches for photographs and history, and it returns proposals with their sources. It cannot change the archive itself; I decide what goes in.

What came out

The historic archive on the Photos page, 65 items so far, each with its credit or source.

No source, no caption.

Verdict
  • Worked well: finding material and drafting captions, and the split between a helper that proposes and a person who approves.
  • Needed me: judging whether a source is good enough, and saying no.

04Identity, stories and paperwork

Tool
ChatGPT
When
2025
Status
Done

The job

A railroad needs a name, a logo, a voice, and a surprising amount of paper.

What AI did

The logo is AI-generated. The fictional short stories that give the railroad its people were drafted with AI. ChatGPT also generated just about all of the early document files, which is a lot of paper for one hobbyist.

What came out

The railroad's look and backstory, and a pile of documents that later had to be sorted, which is part of why the one repository came about.

Verdict
  • Worked well: getting a first version of everything onto the page fast.
  • Watch for: volume. It is easy to produce more documents than anyone will keep in step.
