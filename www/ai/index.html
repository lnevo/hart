The Diverging Route — AI in Model Railroading

Model railroading is many hobbies in one. A single layout asks you to be a historian and a carpenter, an electrician and a signal engineer, a programmer and a photographer, and in the end a railroader running trains with friends. On a real railroad, the diverging route is the other way through a switch, where a train leaves the straight main line and heads somewhere new. This section is about taking that route: bringing AI into each of those crafts, often in ways the hobby hasn’t tried before, and seeing how much it changes what one modeler can research, build, wire and run.

It is catalogued by topic: the research, the benchwork, the electronics, the signals, the paperwork and the locomotives. Each job says what I asked for, which tool did it, what came out, and an honest verdict on how well it went.

For the story in the order it happened, read Bringing the Railroad Together, written in October 2026. This section is the reference, and it keeps growing as I find new routes to try.

How I use it

A few habits run through every topic. They came from mistakes as much as from plans.

  • It suggests, I approveReadings of car numbers, research finds and changes to the railroad's files come to me as proposals. Nothing reaches the layout until I have looked.
  • The files are the masterEvery computer gets the railroad from one repository. A change made somewhere else is brought back in, checked and only then sent out again.
  • Written down, or it didn't happenAn AI remembers nothing between sessions. Decisions are kept as short records it can read, saying what was chosen and why.
  • Checked against the real thingRules about JMRI, CATS and STS are checked against their source code. Heights, pockets and speeds are checked with calipers, test prints and laps of the loop.
  • No source, no captionA historic photo or fact goes on the site only with its credit or source.
  • Stock where possibleWhen JMRI or CATS does something awkward, the fix goes into the railroad's own data, not into a patched copy of the program.
