How the dispatcher panels grew

The Layout Editor panel, the two CTC panels built over it, CATS and the JMRI lever machine, and the generated system schematic, each shown a step at a time.

HART has two dispatcher panels over the same railroad (see How the signals work). Neither was drawn in one go. Each grew from a few track pieces and station names into the full plant, and the screenshots taken along the way make a short film. Underneath both is JMRI's Layout Editor panel, the drawing of the track that JMRI itself works from, so its film comes first. A fourth film, the system schematic, closes the page. All four loop, with no sound.

The Layout Editor panel

The Layout Editor panel is JMRI's own drawing of the track: every block, turnout and signal mast the software knows about sits on it, and the dispatcher panels are built on top of it. It began in May 2026 as one long, flat schematic with the switches numbered 100 to 115, and was redrawn in June with named tracks and the loop at the east end. By the end of August it carried the signals, the occupancy and the labels used today. It is kept as a picture of the real layout rather than a tidy diagram.

  1. 20 May: the first panel, one long schematic with switch numbers 100 to 115
  2. 7 June: redrawn with named tracks, the loop, and Brick, Plane, East End and Princess
  3. 20 August: signals and occupancy; Barn and Switches 116 to 119
  4. 31 August: SW numbers, block labels and status lamps

The JMRI lever machine

This is the stock JMRI CTC machine, a Union Switch & Signal style panel of levers and code buttons over a gold track board. It was built by scripts rather than by hand: Python code running inside JMRI creates the fifteen control points and their locking, and a separate generator draws the track diagram. The pilot was only two locations, Brick and Plane, three columns wide. The rest followed over a single long session, from the evening of 19 August into the early hours of 20 August, and the finished panel carries the railroad's name across the top. It kept changing after that: it was redrawn by the generator, straightened out with the OS track names, and by late September it was twenty columns wide and used the public track names.

The system schematic

The system schematic is the newest panel. It draws staging, the main level and the upper level on one Letter-landscape sheet, one band per level. Lettered connectors join the levels and the helix. The main line runs straight through every switch, the yard ladders step down diagonally, and the signal masts on staging are drawn in the NYAMR Physical Characteristics Book style.

It is generated, not hand-drawn. A tool reads the track plan's AnyRail JMRI export (track only), applies a short file of as-built facts, and lays out the sheet by rules. It then checks the drawing for crossing lines and overlapping labels; today there are none of either. The tool is a public plugin and skill, schematic-maps, in the model-railroad-skills repository.

The current sheet is a draft until the main and upper levels are named: the draft system schematic (PDF).

The wider story of how these panels fit into the rest of the project is in Bringing the Railroad Together. The technical write-up of the Digicon panels, covering the panel files, how they bind to JMRI and how the field signal heads are addressed, is in the repository as HART_DIGICON_SYSTEM.md.
