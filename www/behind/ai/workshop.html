01One repository

Tool
Cursor, then Claude
When
August to October 2026
Status
Done

The job

By August 2026 the project was busy and scattered: research in one place, the fiction in its drafts, control software, the traffic simulator, car records, the bridge and the website each on their own. A car renamed in one place might never reach the cards, the website or the simulator.

What AI did

Everything was gathered into one repository, a folder where every change is recorded so anyone can see what changed and go back. Cursor started it; Claude took over the consolidation, tidied the folders and moved the generic knowledge out into public projects. One naming rule now means a switch, signal or track is called the same thing everywhere.

What came out

A public repository that every computer on the railroad is installed from.

Verdict
  • Worked well: the sorting and moving, which is tedious and error-prone by hand.
  • Lesson: it is the single most useful thing I did for AI. Every later job was easier because there was one place to look.

See it: github.com/lnevo/hart

02One source for each fact

Tool

When

In place

The job

Gathering the files into one place was only half of it. The same facts were still written down in many places: a signal's wiring in a spreadsheet, a slide deck and the JMRI tables; a car in the card files, the traffic simulator and Operations Pro; a locomotive in DecoderPro and the command station. Each copy had been typed separately, so they slowly disagreed.

What AI did

For each kind of fact we picked one place that owns it, its system of record, and made everything else a copy built from it. Then AI rebuilt the copies from the owner instead of patching them by hand, and wrote the scripts that keep them that way.

What came out

The wiring documents, the slide decks, the JMRI tables and the website now show the same names and the same wiring, and a car or locomotive is the same thing in every system. A change is made once, where it is owned, and flows out. When a generated file is wrong, the fix goes into the script that made it, never into the output.

Verdict
  • Worked well: the rebuilding. Hundreds of names and pins carried across many files without a typo is exactly the job AI is good at.
  • Needed me: choosing each owner. Once that was decided, the rest was mechanical.
  • Lesson: this, more than any single tool, is why the project stays in step as it grows.

03Agents and skills

Tool
Claude
When
October 2026 on
Status
Five agents, six skills

The job

A general AI knows a little about JMRI, CATS and STS, and guesses the rest. The answers that matter on this railroad, such as why a mast stays red or where a value on the panel comes from, need the real behaviour of those programs and a look at what the layout is reporting.

What AI did

Two things were set up. Skills are written guides the AI loads when a topic comes up, so it works from checked notes instead of memory: editing JMRI panel files safely, Signal Mast Logic, the Dispatcher System, JMRI and MQTT, running CATS on JMRI, and STS in Docker. Every rule was checked against the program’s own source. Two read-only tools let it see what JMRI and the layout’s MQTT messages are reporting right now.

Agents are helpers with one job each. One traces why a signal, panel, Dispatcher run or MQTT value behaves as it does. One researches the railroad’s history and returns proposals with sources. Others build and review this website, or look for stale pages and dead code. The tracer and the researcher can only read, so they can be handed a big search without risk to the railroad.

What came out

Questions about the signals and the panels get answered from the source and the live layout, not from a guess. The skills were general enough to share and became the public model-railroad-skills plugin; the Operations Pro and STS plugins followed the same pattern.

Verdict
  • Worked well: skills checked against the source, so the AI stops describing how JMRI ought to work and says how it does.
  • Watch for: an agent handed my guess tends to come back agreeing with it. Give it the evidence, not the conclusion.

See it: model-railroad-skills on GitHub

The job

The site serves crews on session night, visitors who want the history, and modelers who want to see how it works. It began on a local server for operators only.

What AI did

It built the pages, moved the site to its own domain and keeps it drawn from the same data the railroad uses, so a change is made once. A reviewer agent reads pages as a first-time reader, a historian or a designer would. The build refuses to publish if anything private, such as a local address, has leaked into a page.

What came out

The site you are reading, including this section.

Verdict
  • Worked well: pages that stay in step with the railroad, and reviews from more than one point of view.
  • Needed me: the voice, and saying when a page tries to say too much. That is how this section started.

05Tools shared with others

Tool
Claude
When
October 2026 on
Status
Public

The job

Along the way the AI learned how JMRI, CATS and STS really behave. That knowledge is worth more shared than kept.

What AI did

It packaged what is generic into public projects, none holding anything specific to this railroad. The guides in the skills plugin were checked against each program's own source.

  • model-railroad-skills: the six skills and two read-only tools described above, as a Claude Code plugin.
  • The Operations Pro plugin: sets up, checks and explains an Operations Pro railroad.
  • The STS plugin, in sts-docker: operate and inspect a traffic simulator through its REST interface.
  • jmri-cs105-roster-sync: the JMRI roster on a TCS CS-105.
  • LCOS_ESP32_MQTT_Client: the LCOS bridge.
  • STL Studio: a free web editor for the logic inside an RR-CirKits Tower LCC+Q. You snap blocks together and it writes the exact text the node expects. HART does not use Tower nodes; it was built for the wider LCC community.
Verdict
  • Worked well: writing rules down for a stranger made them better for me too.
  • Rule: nothing specific to this railroad goes into a shared tool. The railroad's own values live in its settings.
