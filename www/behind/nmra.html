How MMR works

  • ConfirmBe a current NMRA member.
  • To doEarn 7 of the 12 certificates, with at least one from each of the four groups below.
  • To doFor each certificate, send a Statement of Qualification (SOQ) with evidence to the Division or Region AP Manager. Models and scenes need a Merit Award (87.5 of 125 points) from a contest or an AP evaluation.

What to do first

  1. Model Railroad Engineer – Electrical. The closest. Staging already has command control, detection, a yard, an engine house and CTC desks. What is left is the schematics and a witness.
  2. Chief Dispatcher. 87 operating hours are already logged, with enough crew and yardmaster time. It needs 10 hours as dispatcher, a freight timetable and a train chart.
  3. Model Railroad Author. The website articles can already give the 21 points allowed from informal work. The other 21 come from newsletter or magazine articles and clinics.
  4. Model Railroad Engineer – Civil. Plan it into the main level, which restarts after Thanksgiving: hand-lay three pieces of trackwork there and ballast 50 ft of finished track.
  5. Master Builder – Cars and Prototype Models. One certificate each from the equipment and setting groups. Neville Island prototype research is already written up for Prototype Models.

Certificate checklists

Model Railroad Engineer – Electrical

Group C6 of 11 done

Build and document the wiring of a layout that runs two trains at once. No merit judging; a witness signs off.

  • DoneRun two mainline trains independently, in both directions. DCC on staging, with the two-track helix and loops (track plan).
  • DoneFive blocks, or the same number of gaps under command control. 57 detection blocks in the tables file.
  • DoneA passing siding.
  • PartlyA reversing loop, wye, turntable or transfer table. The balloon at the main level's east end is planned, not built. Check whether staging already has one.
  • DoneA yard of three or more tracks with its own lead. South Yard, S-R to S-4.
  • DoneStorage for two locomotives. Engine house EH-1 to EH-3.
  • DoneA protected power supply. Block detectors sit behind circuit breakers.
  • PartlyWire three trackwork items (such as a crossover, turnout frogs, a reversing section). Crossovers at Switch 7 and Switch 23. Pick three and describe their wiring.
  • PartlyThree of the 23 control items (a to w). Candidates: turnout position indication, occupancy detection on five blocks, signals, CTC (signals, panels). Some items refuse complete commercial units, so check the LCOS boards against the list.
  • PartlySchematics of the track power circuit and of the six chosen items. Wiring is documented in the wiring viewer and layout documentation. Draw them in the form the SOQ asks for.
  • To doSigned witness certification.

Next: choose the 3 trackwork and 3 control items, draw the six schematics, and ask a member to witness.

Chief Dispatcher

Group C6 of 10 done

Operate on layouts for 50 hours in several jobs, and prepare the paperwork for an operating layout.

  • Done50 operating hours in all. 87 hours logged in 2025 and 2026, over 27 sessions on other layouts.
  • DoneAt least 10 hours in each of two jobs besides dispatcher. 75 hours as conductor, engineer or yard crew; 12 as yardmaster.
  • To doAt least 10 hours as dispatcher. None logged yet. Dispatch at HART sessions, or ask for the dispatcher's chair on visits, and add those hours to the log.
  • DoneA layout that runs two mainline trains and yard switching at the same time. At least five HART sessions run with dispatcher, yardmaster and local jobs.
  • DoneA schematic with distances. Track plan with block lengths, and the station maps.
  • To doA timetable of at least 8 hours, with at least three scheduled mainline trains each way. TT-23 covers the streetcar only.
  • To doA train chart (time and distance graph) showing at least one meet.
  • DoneA system for moving cars. Car cards and waybills from STS (article).
  • DoneCrew paperwork. Crew sheets, yardmaster sequence and DS-01.
  • To doWitnessed Certificate of Operation.

Next: log 10 hours of dispatching, and write a freight timetable for D749, NVL and CK1 and draw its train chart.

Model Railroad Engineer – Civil

Group C2 of 5 done

Draw an original track plan, build a finished stretch of track, and scratch-build three pieces of trackwork.

  • DoneAn original scale track plan with a passing siding, four switching locations, engine servicing, a way to turn locomotives and two trains running at once. Plan 5.3b, drawn on the track plan page.
  • PartlyAt least 50 ft of HO track with roadbed profile, ballast and drainage. Staging is laid and running. Ballast is still to do.
  • DoneSix track features from the list of 19 in that stretch. Passing siding, spurs, crossovers, yard ladder, grade (the helix), engine terminal. Confirm against the NMRA list.
  • To doScratch-build three trackwork items (turnout, crossover, crossing and so on), no commercial frogs, each one run through by a locomotive. The main level is the place to do it.
  • To doWitness certification.

Next: plan three hand-laid turnouts into the main level, and ballast a 50 ft run.

Model Railroad Author

Group Dabout 21 of 42 points

Earn 42 points from published writing, clinics and video. Informal work (web articles, video) may give at most 21.

  • Done21 informal points. A web article scores as a Region article (2 points), so the website's articles already pass 21: prototype history, behind the scenes, The Diverging Route. Videos add 3 points an hour.
  • To do21 points from formal work. Articles: Division 1, Region 2, national magazine or NMRA magazine 3. A prototype scale drawing 2 to 6. Clinics of 30 minutes with a handout: 2 to 6 at NMRA events. At most 21 from Division and club publications.
  • To doRecord and Validation form listing each item.

Next: offer the P&OV and Neville Island history, or the STS car forwarding article, to the Division or Region newsletter, and give one as a clinic.

Association Volunteer

Group Dnot logged

Earn 60 time units of NMRA service, certified by the chair or officer you served.

  • ConfirmCommittee or Division posts: 0.5 to 4 units a month by level.
  • ConfirmLayout tours and open houses: 3 units a day (capped by level). Hosting crews on HART during an NMRA event may count.
  • ConfirmJudging, clinics, membership booths, newsletter issues, Scout merit badge counselling.

Next: list any past NMRA service; offering HART for a Division layout tour is a quick start.

Master Builder – Cars

Group A0 of 8 cars

Build eight cars of at least four types, at least one of them a passenger car.

  • To doFour scratch-built cars (90% of parts your own; trucks, wheels and couplers excluded). Ask the AP Manager whether parts you design and 3D print count as scratch-built.
  • To doFour more superdetailed cars.
  • To doOne passenger car (baggage, express reefer and business cars count). The office car on OCS-1 would fit.
  • To doMerit Award (87.5) on four of the eight.

Next: choose four Neville Island cars to scratch-build, such as P&OV coke hoppers or a slag car.

Master Builder – Prototype Models

Group B1 of 4 done

Model a real scene with at least six models of real equipment or structures, checked against plans or photos.

  • DoneWritten research on the real scene. P&OV, coke, cement and chemical history, archive photos.
  • To doSix models: two types of rolling stock, one railroad structure, one locomotive; at least two scratch-built.
  • To doPlans or photos for each model and for the scene.
  • To doMerit Award for the scene.

Next: pick one scene with good photos, such as the Shenango coke works or the P&OV engine house.
