In January 2025 this railroad was only an idea, and AI came into it very shortly after. At first that meant ChatGPT, helping me research a place and write it down. It grew one job at a time, as the layout kept asking for something new.

This is the short version of that journey. Each chapter has a fuller page in The Diverging Route for anyone who wants the details.

01Research and stories

For the first months AI was a research partner and a writer. ChatGPT helped me pick a real corridor to interpret, an era that suited the equipment I had, and a fictional backstory to sit on top of it. It drew the logo, drafted the short stories, and produced just about every document file, which is a lot of paper for one hobbyist. The track plan was settled in July 2025, and the industry research behind the Prototype pages ran on through the rest of the year.

More: Prototype research

02Code that runs

In August 2025 the AI moved from chat into an editor, Cursor, working in the same files I was. In about ten days it wrote a small client that let a cheap board talk to JMRI. That was the turning point: the result was something I could run, not only something to read.

The client grew into the bridge between JMRI and the layout's LCOS electronics, one of the most involved pieces of the project. A hand-typed sketch of the layout became a wiring inventory that still feeds the wiring pages today.

03Panels, desks and signals

Through the first half of 2026 a pipeline turned the AnyRail track plan into JMRI panels, and in August the dispatcher desks went up beside them. The signals took the most trial and error. Several designs were tried before I settled on JMRI as it comes, with the effort going into the railroad's own data instead. How the dispatcher panels grew shows the desks being redrawn step by step.

04Cards and traffic

The first operating session ran on Post-it flags. Car cards came next, and they became one of the best uses of AI on the whole project: each photo of a car was cropped, its reporting marks read and matched to the roster, and a card printed with its own picture. The same records seeded the traffic simulator, which AI helped extend and tune. I wrote that part up for The Cannon Ball1.

More: Operations · The operations story

05One place

By August 2026 the project was real, busy and scattered. Each piece worked on its own and nothing made sure they agreed, so a car renamed in one place might never reach the cards or the website.

The fix was one repository, a folder where every change is recorded, public at github.com/lnevo/hart. Cursor began the gathering and Claude took it over. A habit came with it: decisions are written down, and every tool reads the same rules. Early on I asked it for documents. Now I give it a place to work and rules to follow. How that repository is laid out today is in Inside the Repository.

06The helix

Meanwhile the biggest physical job was under way: the helix that climbs from staging to the main level. Its radius, grade, clearances and build order were worked out in conversation before any wood was cut. Claude then turned the plan into 47 printed jigs, templates and spacers, and in about a month the helix was built and tracked. For the first time the AI's work was something I could pick up and build with.

More: Construction · The helix page

07Looking back

I still use all three: Claude is taking over from Cursor, and ChatGPT still has its uses. What changed is how much I ask of them. It began with research and documents, then software that reaches the layout, then cards and traffic for operating sessions, a helix built with parts I could hold, and a project organised well enough for all of it to work together.

None of that was planned. Each step came from asking what else this could help with. Some of it was general enough to share, as public tools2 other modelers can use. The main level comes next, and I look forward to finding out what AI will help with there.
