Extra East: HART Railroad

You are the crew on a working day, from the car dumper on Neville Island to the Chartiers Valley branches, on real railroads between 1949 and 1997. Each decision is a rule from the C&E study deck, and the rule is shown after your answer. A wrong answer can cost a mark. At five marks, or after one mistake that ends the run, the trainmaster takes you off the job.

How a shift works
  • Each shift is a list of work from the switching orders, shown on the right with the plants on the island that year. The map shows where your engine is.
  • A wrong call costs time, a mark, or both. Five marks and the trainmaster takes you off the job. Some mistakes end the shift at once.
  • At the end the shift is scored on time and marks, and your misses are listed with links to the study deck.
  • Each shift is a new draw: the work, the events and the order of the answers change from one shift to the next.
  • The island shifts use GCOR's rules; the 1949 Chartiers runs use the 1949 Standard Code. The PC&Y transfer reads yard limits and crew duties by the GCOR and its signals by the Standard Code speed signals. The PIR run reads track warrants, yard limits and crew duties by the GCOR.
  • On the coal extra the map shows the line and the sheet is the time-table and the orders you hold. At each siding you choose how far to run or whether to wait for a train. A wrong choice can cost a collision, which ends the run.
  • No 307 runs on its schedule, and you answer at each stop.
  • The PC&Y transfer and the PIR run have no time-table. The sheet holds the special instructions and the signals you have passed or the warrants you hold.
  • On the PIR run the dispatcher reads each warrant a line at a time. You check its boxes, fill in the blanks and repeat it back. An error costs 2 minutes.
  • Every question, with its answer and rule, is in the answer key.
