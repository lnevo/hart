Extra East: HART Railroad

Work a shift on the railroads of Neville Island and the Chartiers Valley. Each decision tests a rule from the C&E study deck, and the rule is shown after every answer. This is an edition of Extra East; the game fills in what its sources do not say.

On the islandPittsburgh & Ohio Valley · 1979 to 1990

From 1899 to 1994 the Pittsburgh & Ohio Valley switched the east end of Neville Island: ore to the car dumper, coke to the furnace, hot metal in ladles, pig iron for the barge. The jobs, crews, tracks and moves come from Mark Connolly's blog of his years on the P&OV, 1977 to 1991, collected on the Pittsburgh & Ohio Valley page.

Daylight · Monday to Friday · 7:00 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. · 1981

#105, the ore job

The job the senior men held, on the clean end of the plant. Shove ten-car drags of ore off the South Runner to the Shenango car dumper with SW9 #4, and fuel at South Yard between trips.

Daylight · Monday to Friday · January 1984

#110, the furnace job

The east end, a graphite and coke dust area. Furnace coke over the scale to F1, ladles switched into the furnace's order, hot metal out of the ladle house two at a time, and pig iron on E1 for the barge.

4:00 p.m. to midnight · off Wednesday and Thursday · 1987

#205, second trick

East end and west end as the plant needs: the PC&Y interchange, the weigh, Aristech's tank cars on S9 and the empty ladles back to the Y, with the East Runner in the dark.

Midnight to 7:00 a.m. · off Monday and Tuesday · winter, early 1980s

#305, the midnight job

Ten below zero. The night's coke goes to F1 for the morning, C9 freezes solid, and the shift ends the way the midnight crews ended it: the engine on S9 for sand.

The shift changes with the day · August 1979

#115, the relief job

It covers the other jobs' days off. On a Saturday in August 1979 it is the daylight crew, with SW9 #6: an ore drag, tank cars from the chemical plant, and the scale.

As called · 1988

EXTRA

Called off the extra board when the plant is super busy or the scale is down. Water #4 by hand at the scale house, then whatever the trainmaster needs done.

The yard office · December 1990

Yardmaster at South Yard

Run the day from the yard office by the scale: orders for #105 and #110, the weigh, scrap gons for A furnace as it comes down, and the PC&Y transfer. B furnace is in its last days.

Up the ChartiersPennsylvania Railroad · 1949

Runs on the Pennsylvania Railroad's Chartiers Branch, Carnegie to Washington, under the time-table and train orders. The line, stations and junctions come from Railroads of the Chartiers Valley and the sources listed there.

Freight extra · a weekday in 1949 · Carnegie to Arden

Extra 4619 South, the coal extra

Empties up the double track to Houston for the Westland branch, then the single track to Arden for the Manifold branch. Regular passenger trains and another extra share the single track, and the time-table, a meet order and the semaphores at the interlockings decide who goes first.

How a shift works
  • Each shift is a list of work from the switching orders, shown on the right with the plants on the island that year. The map shows where your engine is.
  • A wrong call costs time, a mark, or both. Five marks and the trainmaster takes you off the job. Some mistakes end the shift at once.
  • At the end the shift is scored on time and marks, and your misses are listed with links to the study deck.
  • Each shift is a new draw: the work, the events and the order of the answers change from one shift to the next.
  • The island shifts use GCOR's rules; the Chartiers runs use the 1949 Standard Code.
  • The Chartiers run is worked the same way: the map shows the line, the sheet on the right is the time-table and the orders you hold, and at each siding you choose how far to run or whether to wait for a train. A wrong choice can cost a collision, which ends the run.
  • Every question, with its answer and rule, is in the answer key.
