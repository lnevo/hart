The railroad and its story

HART Railroad
The model railroad you'll be running, and the railroad company in its story. HART imagines what came after the P&OV: its Pittsburgh & Chartiers Valley (P&CV) Division takes over the island work. About HART
HO scale
The size of the models: 1:87, so a 50-foot freight car is a little under 7 inches long. HO is the most common model railroad scale.
Prototype
The real railroad and the real place a model is based on. HART's prototype is Neville Island, in the Ohio River just below Pittsburgh. The Prototype
P&OV
The Pittsburgh & Ohio Valley Railway, the island's own railroad. It switched the island's furnaces, coke ovens and interchange from 1899 until January 31, 1994.
PC&C
Pittsburgh Coke & Chemical, the company that ran the big plant at the island's east end. When it was broken up in the 1960s, its pieces became Shenango, Kosmos, Calgon and Aristech.
POHC
The Pittsburgh & Ohio Central, a railroad that comes over to the island from Scully Yard in McKees Rocks. Its job in a session is the NVL.
CSX
CSX Transportation, one of the big freight railroads in the eastern United States. Its local, D749, works the island from the Demmler / McKeesport side.

People and jobs

Dispatcher
The person who gives trains permission to move on the main line, lines their routes and sets the signals from a CTC panel. Freight waits for the Route 23 streetcar, and the dispatcher keeps it that way.
Yardmaster
The person in charge of South Yard. The yardmaster sorts cars into cuts by color and hands each train its work, so the jobs don't get in each other's way.
D749
The CSX Neville Island Switcher, the local freight from Demmler / McKeesport. It drops its inbound cars at South Yard and takes the outbound block back. Crew sheet
NVL
The Neville Island Turn, the POHC local from Scully Yard. It swaps cars at South Yard, switches the island industries, then takes the return cut back toward McKees Rocks. Crew sheet
CK1
Coke Transfer. Works Shenango Coke Works: picks up the loaded hoppers, weighs them on the track scale and sorts them. CK1 usually calibrates the scale at the start of the session. Crew sheet
Crew sheet
The one-page instructions for your job: its steps in order, the colors you handle and where you work. You get one at the session, on a clipboard. Session paperwork

Railroad words

Throttle
The hand-held controller you run your locomotive with: how fast it goes and which way. HART crews use TCS and NCE wireless throttles, so you can walk with your train.
Turnout, switch
Two names for the same thing: the place where one track splits into two. You "throw" a switch to send the train one way or the other. On HART's panels they are numbered SW-1 to SW-39. There are three ways to throw one: dial its accessory (DCC) address on your throttle, tap its Touch Toggle on the control panel, or let the dispatcher line it from the JMRI screen.
Fascia
The panel along the front edge of the layout, facing the aisle. Crew sheets hang on clipboards there.
Cut
A group of cars coupled together, handed from one train or track to another.
Block
Two meanings. A block of cars is a group all going to the same place, like D749's outbound block. A block of track is a section with a detector, so the signals know whether a train is in it.
Spot
To put a car at the exact place an industry loads or unloads it. Spot numbers are on the industry spot sheets.
Set out
To leave cars on a track for someone else to pick up, such as D749's inbound set-out at South Yard.
Lift
To pick up cars and add them to your train.
Interchange
Where one railroad hands cars to another. On HART, cars come and go through Scully / McKees Rocks (POHC) and Demmler / McKeesport (CSX).
Reporting marks
The letters and number painted on every car, like N&W 32800. They say whose car it is, and they are how you match a car card to the car on the track.
Car card
A card for each car on the roster with its photo, reporting marks and description: what the car is.
Waybill, CC/WB
The waybill goes with the car card (CC/WB, car card and waybill) and says where the car goes next. Its color bar is the car's destination color. Car cards & waybills
Staging
The tracks on the lower level, at the bottom of the helix, that stand in for the rest of the world. A waybill that says STAGING sends the car off the layout through interchange.
CTC
Centralized Traffic Control. The dispatcher lines switches and clears signals from one panel instead of crews doing it by hand. HART has two CTC panels, and only one is in charge at a time. How the signals work

Paperwork codes

Every HART publication has a number. These are the ones you'll see at a session.

HB-01
The New Operator Primer: the island's story, the jobs, the colors and the scale.
YM-01
The Yardmaster Sequence: who gets which cut, in order, by color.
DS-01
The Dispatcher Train List: the lineup, the priority strip and the timetable.
SM-01 to SM-06
The station maps: SM-01 the subdivision diagram, then West Yard (SM-02), South Yard (SM-03), East End (SM-04), Shenango (SM-05) and Barn (SM-06), with track and switch legends.
TT-23
The Route 23 timetable for the "Neville Queen" streetcar. Freight holds until it clears. The real Route 23 trolley line was retired in the early 1950s; running it again is part of the layout's story.
EQ-01
The Scale Operating Instructions: calibrating the track scale and weighing the coke.

Behind the scenes

You won't need these to run a train, but you may hear them around the dispatcher's desk.

LE (Layout Explorer)
The clickable map of the whole railroad on this site. Click any switch or signal mast to see its control point and hardware.
JMRI
Java Model Railroad Interface, the free, open-source software that runs HART. It keeps track of every switch, occupied block and signal.
CATS
Open-source CTC software that runs on top of JMRI. The dispatcher works its panel with a mouse.
Digicon
The style of on-screen CTC panel that HART's CATS desk is drawn in, from Brick and Plane in the west to Princess and the two interchanges in the east.
STS
The Shipper-driven Traffic Simulator, the program behind each session. It turns shipper orders into car moves and prints the waybills. How it works
LCOS
The wireless layout control system from Beagle Bay. Small radio nodes under the layout throw the switches, read the detectors and light the signals. LCOS and the bridge
